🔍 Deepfakes: The Invisible Menace of Our Digital Age

So... what's actually happening?

Deepfakes are synthetic media designed to mimic real people's appearances and voices, sitting at the cutting edge of generative AI. This technology trains algorithms to replicate specific patterns and characteristics from datasets, effectively producing original, yet entirely fabricated, audio or visuals.

UCL labels Deepfakes as "today's foremost cybercrime threat, a menace that's here to stay." Source: UCL

Recent headlines underscore the urgency of this issue:

  • FCC Clamps Down on AI-Generated Robocalls: In a landmark decision, the FCC has outlawed the use of AI-generated voices in robocalls, reinforcing consumer protections against unsolicited calls. This ruling extends to both text and phone communications, spotlighting the evolving landscape of digital consent. Source: FCC Declaratory Ruling (2024)

  • $25 Million Dollar Deepfake Deception: A finance professional was duped into transferring $25 million to fraudsters impersonating their CFO via deepfake technology in a video call. This incident not only highlights the financial risks but also raises profound questions about our grasp on reality and the safeguarding of our innermost thoughts in the digital era. Source: CNN

The blurring lines between reality and fabrication prompt a pivotal inquiry: Who guards the sanctity of our personal realities?

“As programmed intelligence and personas increase, the definition of reality is shifting. Constructed people and identities are already blurring the line between what’s real and what’s fake.” - Source: Radocchia (2019), Forbes

🔍 Advancing the Frontiers of Digital Authenticity

In our pursuit of a safer digital realm, the conversation around source authentication takes center stage. The integration of metadata that verifies whether content is AI-generated or human-crafted is not just a concept; it's a necessity in the fight against deepfakes and misinformation.

  • MIT's Nightshade: This new data poisoning tool lets artists fight back against generative AI. Source: MIT Technology Review

  • OpenAI and Meta add Source Metadata to all AI generated images. The new metadata tagging allows the company and theoretically any user or organization across the web to identify the imagery as having been made with AI tools. Source: VentureBeat

As a technologist tinkerer who happened to develop early IP registering identities of physical and digital objects and humans on blockchain, I have some thoughts: on Audio/Visual source authentication:

  1. Token gated encrypted files

  2. Within the file (human or AI generated) metadata contains all relevant info including smart contracts for usage, IP, royalties

  3. If a malicious person of system accesses the files without proper authentication or payment for license, data poisoning corrupts data rendering it useless (MIT nightshade)
